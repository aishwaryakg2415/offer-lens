    Know the patterns

    Common job scams, and one real offer

    Open any example in the checker to see how Offer Lens explains it.

    Task and commission scams

    Paid “like and rate” tasks that turn into prepaid tasks you must fund yourself. Withdrawals then get blocked.

    • Daily payouts
    • Telegram
    • Registration fee

    Fake cheque and equipment

    You get a cheque to buy home-office equipment from their “approved vendor”. The cheque bounces after you’ve paid.

    • Chat-only interview
    • Cheque
    • Link shortener

    OTP and identity harvesting

    A “background verification” that asks for your OTP, ID cards or bank login. It’s used to take over your accounts.

    • OTP
    • Aadhaar / PAN
    • Urgent

    Payment processing jobs

    You receive money into your own account and pass it on. That makes you a money mule, and your account can be frozen.

    • Use your account
    • Gift cards
    • Secrecy

    Off-platform recruiters

    A plausible role, but from a personal Gmail and pushing you to WhatsApp. Not always a scam, so verify before sharing more.

    • Free email
    • WhatsApp
    • Immediate joining

    What a genuine offer looks like

    Company email, clear duties and requirements, real interview rounds, an official careers portal, and no fees at any stage.

    • Company domain
    • Assessment + interview
    • No fees

    Safety guide

    Verify any offer in five minutes

    The 5-minute routine

    Check the employer, not the message

    1. Search the company yourself. Open its official website from a search engine, not from the link you were sent.
    2. Find the role on its careers page. If the job isn’t listed there, treat it as unverified.
    3. Match the email domain. Real recruiters write from the company’s own domain, not Gmail or a lookalike.
    4. Call HR on a listed number. Use the contact details on the official site to confirm the offer.
    5. Never pay, never share an OTP. No genuine employer charges fees or needs your PIN.

    “Refundable” means you won’t get it back

    A refundable deposit or registration fee is the most common job-scam ask.

    High pay for easy work is bait

    ₹3,000 a day for liking videos isn’t a job. It’s the opening of a task scam.

    Pressure is a warning sign

    “Reply within 24 hours” stops you checking. A real offer survives a day of questions.

    Already paid or shared documents?

    India: call 1930 or visit cybercrime.gov.in. Tell your bank right away.

    Methodology

    Offer Lens evaluates about 40 text signals across 11 risk categories, plus domain registration age, mail-server records and community reports. Signals combine as independent evidence: score = 1 − ∏(1 − weight). Indicators of a genuine hiring process reduce the score by up to 40%. Any request for payment, an OTP, or the movement of money sets a minimum score of 50. With an AI review (Claude or Groq), the final score is 55% rules and 45% AI.
